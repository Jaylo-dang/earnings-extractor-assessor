Chief Executive Officer and Chair Leroy Ball said, “Our third-quarter results reflect continued challenges in the macroeconomic environment. While we are delivering on our commitments to grow volumes and structurally improve our cost position, higher raw material and logistics costs are more than offsetting those benefits in the near term, placing additional pressure on margins and earnings. Our Catalyst transformation program continues to address these headwinds, fundamentally changing how we drive performance improvement across the organization. Performance Chemicals delivered higher sales and adjusted EBITDA over the prior year quarter, while profitability declined in our Railroad and Utility Products and Services and Carbon Materials and Chemicals businesses. At the same time, we generated record year-to-date operating cash flow and free cash flow by taking decisive action to enhance operational efficiency and controlling what we can control. We remain focused on improving margins, generating cash and allocating capital with discipline to build long-term shareholder value.”

•Net sales increased $13.8 million, or 2.8 percent, in the third quarter of 2026 as compared to the prior year quarter, primarily driven by volume increases in residential and industrial wood preservatives, Class I crossties and U.S. utility poles. The increases were offset in part by unfavorable pricing for Class I crossties in the RUPS segment compared to the prior year quarter. Currency conversion had a favorable effect of $6.1 million compared to the prior year quarter.
•Adjusted EBITDA of $58.0 million decreased $12.9 million, or 18.2 percent, as compared to $70.9 million in the prior year quarter. The year-over-year decrease was due primarily to higher raw material and logistics costs. Also, selling, general and administrative expenses were $3.4 million higher year-over-year due mainly to an increase in compensation-related costs and higher technology costs. The increases were partly offset by lower professional services costs.
•Operating cash flow for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, was a record $126.9 million compared to $77.4 million in the prior year period. The increase primarily reflected improved working capital performance and approximately $14 million of pension funding in the prior-year period, partially offset by lower underlying earnings. The improvement in working capital reflected the timing of customer receipts and vendor payments as well as lower inventory. Free cash flow for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, was $92.0 million, also a record, compared to $39.0 million in the prior year period, net of capital expenditures of $34.9 million and $38.4 million, respectively.
•RUPS net sales increased slightly due primarily to higher volumes for crossties and a five percent volume increase in the company's domestic utility pole business, including an acquisition of a western U.S. pole procurement business. These increases were mostly offset by approximately $6.3 million related to the sale of a railroad services business in the third quarter of 2025 and price decreases across multiple markets, particularly for crossties. Foreign currency changes had a favorable impact of $0.9 million on sales compared to the prior year period, mainly from the company's utility pole business in Australia. Adjusted EBITDA decreased due to lower sales prices, particularly for Class I crossties, $3.7 million of higher raw material and selling, general and administrative costs and approximately $0.6 million related to the sale of the railroad services business. These decreases were partly offset by an increase in sales volume, primarily in the Class I crossties business.

•PC net sales increased due to a ten percent volume growth, primarily in the Americas, related to market share gains and inventory restocking. Lower prices in Europe were mostly offset by higher prices in the Americas and Australasia. Foreign currency changes had a favorable impact of $2.6 million on sales compared to the prior year period. Adjusted EBITDA increased due primarily to higher sales volume and favorable utilization, partly offset by higher raw material costs of $4.7 million. Raw material costs were unfavorably impacted primarily by increased scrap copper costs, net of the benefit realized from the company's copper-hedging program.
•CMC net sales decreased due mainly to reduced volumes, particularly for carbon pitch. These decreases were partly offset by price increases for carbon pitch and carbon black feedstock. Carbon pitch prices were approximately four percent higher globally from the prior year period, driven by market dynamics in North America and Europe. Foreign currency changes had a favorable impact of $2.6 million on sales compared to the prior year period. Adjusted EBITDA decreased due to higher raw material costs of $9.0 million and lower utilization of $1.7 million, primarily in North America, due to feedstock quality issues and the accelerated closure of the company's Stickney facility.

2026 Outlook
The company continues to expect positive volume trends and increasing benefits from its ongoing operational improvement efforts. However, higher than anticipated raw material and logistics costs, partially influenced by elevated energy prices, have created additional margin pressures that are expected to persist through the remainder of 2026. While the company has made strong progress on the planned actions to optimize its production network, these benefits are not expected to fully offset the near-term impact of higher input costs and weaker end market demand. At the same time, several working capital initiatives are anticipated to generate additional cash flow, supporting the company's ability to maintain its operating cash flow guidance. Accordingly, Koppers is updating its 2026 forecast as follows:

Raw material and logistics costs have escalated throughout the year faster than we can recover in the near term. However, commercial actions that take effect in 2027 will help reset earnings to a meaningfully higher level as we are beginning to realize the benefits of several large self-help initiatives, including the accelerated Stickney closure and the idling of our Florence treating plant. About Koppers
Koppers (NYSE: KOP) is an integrated global provider of essential treated wood products, wood preservation technologies and carbon compounds. Our team of approximately 1,700 employees create, protect and preserve key elements of our global infrastructure – including railroad crossties, utility poles, outdoor wooden structures, and production feedstocks for steel, aluminum and construction materials, among others – applying decades of industry-leading expertise while constantly innovating to anticipate the needs of tomorrow. Together we are providing safe and sustainable solutions to enable rail transportation, keep power flowing, and create spaces of enjoyment for people everywhere. Protecting What Matters, Preserving The Future. Learn more at Koppers.com.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures
This press release contains certain non-GAAP financial measures. Koppers believes that adjusted EBITDA, adjusted net income, adjusted earnings per share, free cash flow and net debt provide information useful to investors in understanding the underlying operational performance of the company, its business and performance trends, and facilitates comparisons between periods. The exclusion of certain items permits evaluation and a comparison between periods of results for ongoing business operations, and it is on this basis that Koppers management internally assesses the company’s performance. In addition, the Board of Directors and executive management team use adjusted EBITDA as a performance measure under the company’s annual incentive plans and for certain performance share units granted to management prior to 2026. The Board of Directors and executive management also use free cash flow and adjusted earnings per share as performance measures for certain performance share units granted to management in 2026.
Although Koppers believes that these non-GAAP financial measures enhance investors’ understanding of its business and performance, these non-GAAP financial measures should not be considered an alternative to GAAP basis financial measures and should be read in conjunction with the relevant GAAP financial measure. Other companies in a similar industry may define or calculate these measures differently than the company, limiting their usefulness as comparative measures. Because of these limitations, these non-GAAP financial measures should not be considered in isolation or as substitutes for performance measures calculated in accordance with GAAP.
